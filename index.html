Chapter 02 — Methodology

How the sky becomes drinking-grade water.

Six stages, zero electricity. Rainwater is caught, guided, cleaned and stored using gravity as the only pump — a principle used since 3000 BCE in Rajasthan's stepwells, re-engineered here with modern materials.

STEP / 01

Catchment

The rooftop is the catchment area. A 100 m² roof intercepts ~79,000 L in an average Delhi monsoon year.

STEP / 02

Conveyance

PVC gutters and downpipes channel runoff with a 1% slope — no pumps, no energy, no moving parts.

STEP / 03

First-Flush Diverter

The first 10–15 minutes of rain carry roof dust and droppings. A diverter discards it automatically, protecting the filter.

STEP / 04

3-Layer Filtration

Pebbles strain debris, sand strips silt by depth filtration, cotton polishes clarity. 99.6% of turbidity gone.

STEP / 05

Storage

A food-grade HDPE tank holds the harvest. An overflow line routes surplus to a recharge pit for the water table.

STEP / 06

Use & Recharge

Gardening, flushing, washing — 45% of household demand. Every litre reused is a litre not pumped from the ground.

Chapter 03 — The Algorithm

One filter, eight languages.

    Chapter 06 — Future Scope & Conclusion

    Where AquaHarvest goes next.

    The prototype proves the physics. The roadmap makes it smart, potable and community-scale.

    NEXT / 01

    IoT Sensing

    Ultrasonic level sensors, TDS probes and GSM alerts report tank status to any phone — no app install, just an SMS.

    NEXT / 02

    AI Rain Prediction

    A lightweight ML model on IMD forecast data pre-emptively empties tanks before storms and times the first-flush cycle.

    NEXT / 03

    Potable Upgrade

    Activated carbon + UV stage lifts the harvest to full BIS drinking standard, cutting TDS and pathogens entirely.

    NEXT / 04

    Community Scale

    Shared recharge pits for schools and housing societies — one monsoon can raise a local water table measurably.

    The monsoon is not the problem. Letting it escape is.

    AquaHarvest proves that a ₹15,000 rig and high-school physics can turn any roof into a 67,000-litre annual reservoir — paying for itself in seven months while recharging the groundwater beneath it. Water security doesn't need to wait for mega-projects. It can start with the next rainfall, on your own roof.
