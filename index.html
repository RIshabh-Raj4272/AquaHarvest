The idea

Rain becomes a resource.

Instead of allowing rainwater to flow away, the model captures water from a house roof and prepares it for useful, non-drinking purposes such as gardening.

☁

Collect

The sloped roof directs rainfall into a gutter and downpipe, beginning the harvesting process.

◫

Filter

Pebbles, sand, big stones, and cotton remove leaves, dirt, particles, color, and some odors.

♻

Reuse

Filtered water is stored in a tank and reused for plants, reducing dependence on tap water.

How it works

One continuous journey.

From the first drop on the roof to the final drop in the garden, every component has a clear purpose.

01

Roof catchment

Rain falls onto the roof, which acts as the main collection surface.

02

Gutter and pipe

A gutter catches the runoff and carries it safely through a downpipe.

03

Natural filter

Water passes through four layers that capture impurities of different sizes.

04

Storage and use

The treated water enters a covered tank and is released when the garden needs it.

Natural filtration

Cleaned.
Layer by layer.

Each material performs a different job. Together they improve the water's clarity before it enters the storage tank.

  • 1 Pebbles stop leaves and large debris.
  • 2 Sand captures smaller suspended particles.
  • 3 Charcoal helps reduce color and odors.
  • 4 Cotton catches fine remaining particles.

Why it matters

Small system.
Meaningful impact.

Save fresh water for where it matters most.

Harvested rainwater can replace treated tap water for garden irrigation, cleaning, and other suitable non-drinking uses.

💧

Reduce runoff.

Capturing roof water can reduce puddling, soil erosion, and the amount of stormwater flowing into drains.

☂

Support plants.

Stored rainwater provides a practical backup supply for watering plants during dry days.
